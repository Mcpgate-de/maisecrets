# Benchmark V1 snapshot: maisecrets 0.7.3, 2026-10-21

A new measurement point of Benchmark V1 (`docs/BENCHMARK-V1.md`). It does not replace the baseline of 0.6.39
(`bench/results/2026-10-19-v0.6.39.md`, `docs/BENCHMARKS.md`); it adds 0.7.3 beside it. Every number below comes from
the raw results in `bench/results/2026-10-21-v0.7.3/`; the tables hold counts, never a value.

|---|---|

| Benchmark | Benchmark V1 at commit `93e46e05`, the commit of the 0.6.39 baseline; the tree was clean for every run (`bench_dirty: false` in each result) |
| Other scanners | detect-secrets 1.5.0, secret-scan 0.4.2 (`7d8b5c18`), pii-anon 1.7.0rc1 (`2171ace5`), Preserve 0.6.1 (`01dbd771`): the pins of `bench/adapters/requirements/` |

Four blocks, kept apart:

1. **Benchmark V1, reproduced** (the historical comparison and the market comparison at the V1 versions).
2. **The current versions of the other tools** (a separate block; only Preserve has a new version).
3. **An independent check** on Privy, as totals only (Benchmark V2 at its commit `9fe68d25`); not part of V1.
4. **The opportunity funnel** of what 0.7.3 still misses.

## Answers

1. **0.7.3 against 0.6.39.** On the V1 data, strict PII recall rises on every dataset: Nemotron 0.484 → 0.593,
   Gretel 0.216 → 0.283, Mendeley 0.479 → 0.685, PIILO 0.085 → 0.094; whole addresses 0.113 → 0.475, whole names
   0.109 → 0.223, structured PII 0.815 → 0.907, secrets 0.455 → 0.513. Precision rises on Gretel (0.821 → 0.909) and
   falls on Mendeley (0.9998 → 0.990) and PIILO (0.963 → 0.922). The bank and the catalogs do not move. **On Privy,
   which no rule was written from, the change is small:** recall 0.234 → 0.240, precision 0.441 → 0.448, and names
   312 → 312 whole of 8,121. Most of the gain is in the forms of the development datasets.
2. **Against the others (samples of 1 in 50, the same cases for each):** maisecrets has the highest precision on
   every PII dataset (0.927 to 1.000 against 0.22 to 0.84) and the highest F1 on Gretel (0.456 against 0.296 and
   0.289) and Mendeley (0.808 against 0.704 and 0.542); on Nemotron its F1 is level with Preserve (0.737 against
   0.744). It has the lowest PII recall on Nemotron and Gretel. On secrets it leads on every source.
3. **Where maisecrets leads:** secrets (catalogs 0.586 against 0.235 at most; Nemotron 0.441 against 0.170 at most;
   the bank 0.941 against 0.765), whole addresses (Gretel 177 of 645 against 39 and 3; Mendeley 574 of 894 against
   0 and 0), national ids, and precision everywhere.
4. **About level:** emails, IPs, IBANs, cards, dates of birth and user names; phone numbers (Preserve slightly ahead);
   Nemotron overall (F1 0.737 against 0.744).
5. **Weaker:** names. Whole names on the samples: Gretel 189 of 1,409 (Preserve 713, pii-anon 650), Mendeley 273 of
   1,485 (pii-anon 1,450), Nemotron 743 of 2,696 (pii-anon 2,080, Preserve 1,777); Privy 8 of 191 against 54 for
   Presidio. Also partial and missed addresses in running text, and PIILO (names of students, recall 0.094).
6. **Deterministic potential left:** of 320,586 spans still partial or missed on the four PII datasets, 44.6 % sit in
   buckets a rule could reach at all (A+B 117,229, C 25,745); the reading of 2026-10-20 put the part without a real
   false-alarm risk (A) at about 1,500 on `dev`, and the part a rule can take without tipping precision at 25,000 to
   30,000 on `dev` (about 13 to 16 % of what is left there). The 4,926 catalog spans are token formats, one rule each.
7. **NER:** yes, as the next step for names, as an optional layer. 48.7 % of what is left (156,166) is D, and 96 %
   of D are names in running text with no cue. The rules of 0.7.x did not move names on Privy; Presidio with its NER
   model finds about seven times as many names there (54 against 8 of 191 on the sample). Whether to add a model is the maintainer's decision (a dependency, a
   second layer); this snapshot only measures.
8. **Strong enough for a public comparison?** The measurement is reproducible: the same case hashes as the
   baseline on all 39 result files, and every result of the other four scanners identical case by case to the run
   of 2026-10-19. As a ranking it is biased toward maisecrets: the four datasets and the bank are development data
   (the rules of 0.6.40 to 0.7.3 were written by reading their `dev` split), the other tools were not tuned to them,
   and the samples are 1 in 50. A public comparison should show the V1 tables next to the Privy totals and say this;
   the V1 numbers alone overstate maisecrets.

## Opportunity funnel

`bench/funnel.py` puts each span 0.7.3 leaves partial or missed into one bucket by the text around it, and each bucket
into a category. The buckets and the mapping come from the frontier block of 2026-10-20 and were fixed before this
run (the script's docstring). The four PII datasets in full, all splits; `funnel.csv` has every row.

| Category | Buckets | Spans | Share | Largest clusters |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| A deterministic, low risk | not separable by context alone | about 1,500 on `dev` (reading of 2026-10-20) | < 1 % | a value after its own label that the label rule's check refuses; names with an inner capital |
| B deterministic, false-alarm risk | label-same, person-cue, prose of a class with a shape | 117,229 (with A) | 36.6 % | names after a cue word 42,372; addresses in running text 40,122; phones in running text 8,223; user names in running text 7,971; names after a name label 6,623; addresses after an address label 4,922 |
| C structural / parser | record-field | 25,745 | 8.0 % | names in record fields 16,365; addresses 2,849; user names 2,413; ids 1,172; dates of birth 1,021 |
| D likely NER / semantic | prose of a name, prose of a secret | 156,166 | 48.7 % | names in running text without a cue 150,653; passwords 2,818 and keys 2,695 in a sentence |
| E dataset / label | placeholder | 9,439 | 2.9 % | placeholder names 7,557; placeholder id digits 1,638; plus the unlabelled addresses of Mendeley counted as false positives above |
| F unclear | label-other | 12,007 | 3.7 % | names after another label 9,145; addresses after another label 1,708 |

By dataset: Nemotron 142,783, Mendeley 81,441, Gretel 95,007, PIILO 1,355. On `dev` alone the funnel has 191,602
spans in the same proportions (A+B 70,018, C 15,446, D 93,271, E 5,682, F 7,185). The catalogs leave 4,926 token
spans partial or missed: formats of other catalogs, each a rule of its own, not counted above.

How much is realistic is an estimate from the reading of 2026-10-20 on `dev`, not a measurement: B 15,000 to 20,000,
C 7,000 to 10,000, A about 1,500; D close to nothing without a model; E nothing by policy.

## Method

- **Datasets and splits:** as V1: the bank by its `dev` and `val` split; each PII dataset `--split all`, maisecrets
  in full and every scanner on the fixed sample `--every 50` (sha256 of the case id); the catalogs `--every 1`.
- **Commands:**

  ```bash
  git worktree add --detach ../bench-v1 93e46e05          # the benchmark
  git worktree add --detach ../ms-0.7.3 v0.7.3            # the release under test
  cd ../bench-v1
  BENCH_PYTHON=… BENCH_DETECT_SECRETS_PYTHON=… BENCH_SECRET_SCAN_PYTHON=… BENCH_PII_ANON_PYTHON=… \
    BENCH_PRESERVE_PYTHON=… BENCH_CATALOGS=… bash bench/run_all.sh OUT ../ms-0.7.3
  python3 scripts/measure_fp_bank.py measure CACHE --json OUT/fpbank-7trees.json     # in ../ms-0.7.3
  python3 bench/history.py BASELINE_DIR OUT > history.md                              # in this tree
  python3 bench/funnel.py OUT --maisecrets ../ms-0.7.3 --csv funnel.csv nemotron gretel mendeley piilo
  ```

  Each environment is built from `bench/adapters/requirements/<tool>.txt` with `pip install --no-deps -r`.
- **Case hashes** (first 12 of the sha256; the full value is in each result file): bank dev `6fe1029ba80e`, bank val
  `7d350302c12c`; the others are listed in `bench/results/2026-10-21-v0.7.3/v1/report.md` per section.
- **Scoring:** unchanged V1 (`docs/BENCHMARK-V1.md`, "When a span counts as found" and "Metrics").
- **Raw results:** `bench/results/2026-10-21-v0.7.3/` holds the JSON of every run as gzip (no time stamp),
  `environment.json`, the logs, the report, `history.md`, `funnel.csv` and `SHA256SUMS`; the baseline's raw results
  are in `bench/results/2026-10-19-v0.6.39/` (the holdout run is not published). Local paths are replaced by
  `<venvs>` and `<checkouts>`.

## Limitations

- **Development data.** The four datasets and the bank are the data the rules of 0.6.40 to 0.7.3 were written from
  (their `dev` split was read; `val` decided). V1 measures how well the rules fit these forms; Block 3 shows how
  little of it carries to Privy.
- **Samples.** The comparison with other tools runs on 1 in 50; a class with fewer than 30 spans has no rate.
- **Scope.** Each tool is scored on what it claims; an overall rank across tools with different aims is not given.
- **Labels.** Mendeley leaves some addresses of persons unlabelled; the 1,673 false positives of the two-line rule are
  counted as such.
- **Precision definition.** A finding on any sensitive span counts as right; a finding that is too wide is counted
  apart (`too wide`), not as a false positive.
- **Environment manifest.** `environment.json` says the benchmark tree was modified: the manifest script (added after
  V1) was copied into the V1 tree to write it, then removed. The runs themselves record `bench_dirty: false`.
- **A tool fault found during the run.** `scripts/compare_bench.py` given two result files (not their directories)
  printed "0 finding(s)": it found no results and compared nothing. It now refuses such an argument (exit 2). No
  number of this snapshot or of an earlier one depends on it: the comparisons above ran on directories.
